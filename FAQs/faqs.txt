About Salman — FAQs
Basic

Who is Salman?
Salman is a software developer from Lahore who is building his career around backend engineering, AI, and continuous learning. He is also someone who spends a lot of time thinking about personal growth, purpose, consistency, books, and how to build a meaningful life alongside a career.

Where is Salman from?
Salman is based in Lahore, Pakistan.

What does Salman do?
He works primarily as a Python/backend developer, while gradually moving deeper into AI engineering and modern backend systems.

What did Salman study?
He studied Computer Science and graduated from COMSATS University in 2023.

How would Salman describe himself professionally?
As someone who is still growing but wants to become a genuinely strong engineer rather than simply collect technologies.

Personality

What is Salman like as a person?
He is relatively introverted, thoughtful, curious, and reflective. He tends to think deeply about things rather than immediately accepting them.

Is Salman an introvert?
Yes. He generally prefers quieter environments and meaningful one-to-one or small-group conversations over large social gatherings.

Does Salman enjoy meeting new people?
Yes, but he prefers low-pressure environments. He has particularly enjoyed non-technical meetups where conversations happen naturally rather than formal networking.

What kind of conversations does Salman enjoy?
Conversations about books, ideas, life, personal growth, technology, philosophy, experiences, and people's perspectives.

Is Salman a very social person?
Not particularly. He seems more comfortable with depth than quantity when it comes to relationships and conversations.

Does Salman overthink?
At times, yes. One of his recurring personal goals is learning to act consistently without overthinking everything.

What is something Salman struggles with?
Maintaining consistency without turning every goal into something overwhelming or excessively complicated.

What does Salman value?
Growth, learning, independence, meaningful work, consistency, stability, authenticity, and understanding things deeply.

Learning & Growth

Is Salman naturally curious?
Very much so. He tends to explore topics beyond what is immediately required for his job.

How does Salman like to learn?
He prefers understanding something by actually building with it. He generally dislikes blindly copying solutions without understanding what is happening.

Does Salman like structured learning?
Yes. He often benefits from clear roadmaps, phases, checklists, and practical projects.

Does Salman like learning everything at once?
No. He has increasingly recognized that trying to learn too many things simultaneously creates unnecessary overwhelm.

What is Salman currently trying to improve about himself?
Consistency, technical depth, communication, career progression, and the ability to keep moving forward without constantly questioning whether he is doing enough.

What does Salman mean by “growth”?
Not simply learning more technologies. For him, growth also means becoming more capable, independent, confident, disciplined, and thoughtful.

Career

What does Salman want from his career?
He wants to become a strong software/backend engineer and eventually move toward senior-level engineering and AI engineering responsibilities.

Is salary important to Salman?
Yes. He wants significant career and salary growth, but not at the expense of becoming technically competent.

Does Salman want to remain a Python developer forever?
Python is an important part of his career, but his larger goal is to become a strong engineer rather than attach his identity permanently to one programming language.

What kind of engineer does Salman want to become?
A backend/AI engineer who understands systems deeply enough to design, build, debug, deploy, and maintain real products.

Does Salman prefer practical work or theory?
Practical work, but he wants enough theory to understand why something works.

What does Salman dislike about learning technology?
Learning things purely because they are trendy, copying code without understanding it, and accumulating tutorials without actually building anything.
